🏛️ Babylonian Origins of Trigonometry

Trigonometry originated from the ancient Babylonian civilization, around 1800 BCE. Babylonian astronomers and mathematicians were the first to study angles and their relationships in triangles.

They used sophisticated mathematical techniques to:

  • Track celestial bodies and stars
  • Calculate distances without direct measurement
  • Develop early astronomy and calendar systems

🔢 The Sexagesimal (Base-60) System

The Babylonians used a sexagesimal system — a base-60 counting system, not the base-10 we use today.

This ancient system still influences us:

  • 60 seconds in a minute
  • 60 minutes in an hour
  • 360 degrees in a circle (6 × 60)

The Babylonians divided angles into 60 equal parts. Since 6 triangles can form a complete circle, they established that 1 circle = 6 × 60 = 360 degrees.

📐 Right Triangle: The Three Sides

When we study one angle (other than the right angle) in a right triangle, we identify three sides:

Hypotenuse (H)

The longest side, opposite the right angle

Opposite (O)

The side opposite to the angle θ we're studying

Adjacent (A)

The side next to the angle θ (not the hypotenuse)
